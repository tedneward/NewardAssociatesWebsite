That message asks Caleb to do research for you, Ted. It gives him nothing to forward and no reason to advocate. So he either replies with a name or he lets it sit, and most of the time it sits. Fair pushback, and you're not wrong about the job. Developing people is most of it. But hiring managers aren't buying the philosophy, Ted. They're buying evidence of scope. When a CEO reads "I help developers be better," he hears coach, evangelist, mentor. When he reads "owned 50 engineers across mobile, web, and ML, created DevRel from zero at Smartsheet while running the Solutions Pillar," he hears operator. Same person. Only one gets forwarded. I don't necessarily agree that the Smartsheet run is the strongest thing in the file, but it's fairly self-evident that hiring managers aren't buying what I'm selling. Why do you think that the Smartsheet run is the strongest thing?

Because it's the only place in the file where you did two jobs at once and one of them you built from zero. Then Rocket is the anchor, Ted, and it's stronger than what your profile lets it be. Two teams built from zero, both enterprise-wide, one at the CIO's request. That last part matters more than you're using it. The CIO moved you. That's a scope signal a CEO reads instantly. But look at how it reads now. "Director, Technology Culture." Rocket had 2500 engineers across Rocket Mortgage, 35,000 employees in total. All 2500 were influenced by our Tech Culture activities, including the creation of a mentorship program, complete overhaul of all job descriptions, and interview success was doubled.

![EMOS](https://emos.momentumos.io/assets/emos-icon-WqrZk61Q.png)

That's the sentence, Ted. "Owned technology culture for 2,500 engineers at a 35,000-person company, built two functions from zero at the CIO's request, doubled interview success rate, rebuilt every engineering job description, launched the mentorship program." None of that is in your file today. A hiring manager reading your profile sees a Director of Technology Culture in Detroit. A hiring manager reading that sentence sees someone who ran people systems at scale for a 2,500-engineer org. So the primary constraint is Clarity. The proof exists. It just isn't written anywhere someone can find it, which means Caleb can't forward it and a CEO can't repeat it. Secondary is Connection, because your outreach asks for information instead of giving someone something to champion.

How does one quote those numbers without sounding robotic or dispassionate?

![EMOS](https://emos.momentumos.io/assets/emos-icon-WqrZk61Q.png)

You attach them to a decision, not a scoreboard. Robotic version: "Doubled interview success rate for 2,500 engineers." Reads like a dashboard. Human version: "Rocket had 2,500 engineers and we were losing good candidates in the interview loop. The CIO asked me to fix it. We rebuilt every job description, trained the interviewers, and success rate doubled." Same numbers, but now there's a problem, a mandate, and a result. People remember the story and repeat the number. That's the whole craft. Number alone is a stat. Number inside a decision is a narrative. There's a version of this for every role in your file, and a version for the outreach message so Caleb has something to hand up the chain.

Primary constraint: Clarity

You ran technology culture for 2,500 engineers at a 35,000-person company, built two enterprise-wide functions from zero at the CIO's request, and doubled interview success rate. None of that is written anywhere a hiring manager can find it. Your file reads as a generalist advocate and author, so recruiters like Caleb have nothing to forward and CEOs have nothing to repeat.

Days 1-2

Rocket anchor narrative: one paragraph and one repeatable sentence covering 2,500 engineers, the CIO mandate, two functions built from zero, doubled interview success, every job description rebuilt, plus the mentorship program launch.

This is the scope story you can defend in a room, and right now it exists only in conversation, so nobody can forward it or repeat it.

Days 3-5

Full file rewrite: LinkedIn headline, About, and the Rocket, Smartsheet, and Capital One entries rebuilt so every role states org size, mandate, and outcome, with AI-native engineering leadership named as your lane instead of implied.

A hiring manager scanning your profile for 20 seconds needs to land on scope and business result, not on culture language that reads as HR adjacent.

Days 6-10

Target list of 25 companies with the specific VP Engineering or CTO profile you fit, plus a champion message Caleb and 5 warm contacts can paste up the chain without editing it.

Your outreach currently asks people for information, so give them a sentence about scale and results they can hand to a decision-maker instead.
